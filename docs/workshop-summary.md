# Workshop Summary

## Identity

THE ARCHITECTURE OF SHARED MEANING: Aligning Human Minds and AI Intelligence Through the C.L.E.A.R.+ Protocol is a 120-minute professional workshop about making intent executable for humans and AI.

## Teaching Spine

1. Participants feel the Assumption Gap through opening examples and the Mars Climate Orbiter case.
2. Participants experience failed delegation in the Architect vs. Builder lab.
3. Participants see how role and audience change meaning in the Can we climb a tree? multi-lens experiment.
4. Participants learn C.L.E.A.R.+ as the shared protocol.
5. Participants reset, select real examples, and return with purpose.
6. Participants refactor executive requests in triads using a six-point rubric.
7. Participants run Diagnose -> Isolate -> Constrain with zero-trust data governance.
8. Participants teach back the method and commit to one real use.

## Theoretical Foundations

- Clark & Brennan: common ground, feedback, and repair explain why instructions need shared context.
- Chris Voss: calibrated questions and explicit constraints reveal hidden assumptions.
- Nancy Duarte: strong openings, contrast, and Peak-End closes shape retention.
- Vaswani et al.: attention-based models respond to available context, so prompt framing and verification matter.

## Operational Rules

- Start with participant goals and friction points.
- Keep public slides free of facilitator controls and raw notes.
- Use `s` to open Presenter View and `Escape` to close it.
- Keep the 00:50-01:00 break intact.
- Prefer participant-owned cases over generic examples after the break.
- Require at least one negative constraint and one verification check in production prompts.
- In Segment 7, change one variable per iteration.

## Scenario Inventory

- Mars Climate Orbiter assumption gap.
- Tree-climbing safety instruction.
- Interpersonal conflict resolution message.
- Shared-responsibility handoff.
- Can we climb a tree? lenses: child, psychologist, risk officer, arborist, heavy-eared alien.
- Executive scenario library: board update, customer apology, hiring calibration, risk memo, product launch, budget tradeoff, incident review, policy rewrite, sales brief, research synthesis, vendor comparison, executive ghostwrite.

## Success Criteria

- Participants can explain C.L.E.A.R.+ in one minute.
- Participants can identify which field caused a prompt failure.
- Participants can rewrite one real prompt with explicit exclusions and pass-fail verification.
- Participants leave with a committed use case and an accountability path.
